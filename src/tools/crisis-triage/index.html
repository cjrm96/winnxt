Rapid response

Something just happened. Ten questions, one at a time, and you get a straight answer: whether to respond at all, where, how, and in what order.

Before anything else: don't post while you're angry, and don't delete anything that's already public. Those two mistakes cause more damage than most of the things people panic about.

Nothing you type here is saved anywhere. Not to a server, not to this browser, not to a file. Close the tab and it's gone, so print the report before you leave. That's also why you can afford to be blunt with it.

Rules, not vibes

Your answers run through a fixed decision model. Nothing here is generated, guessed, or improvised. The same answers always produce the same read, and every branch traces back to something published.

  • Established crisis research. Situational Crisis Communication Theory for matching your response to how much blame you actually carry. Misinformation research for correcting a false claim without spreading it further. Studies on disclosure timing for when to get ahead of a story.
  • Real cases that already ran this experiment. Every outcome comes with people who faced the same decision: what they chose, how the public reacted, and where it ended up. Political and corporate both, because the mechanics do not care which you are. Tylenol, Watergate, the Streisand effect, campaigns that answered too slowly and campaigns that got out in front.
  • Local reality. Built for local scale, where it starts in one Facebook group or one thread and the whole thing is decided in hours rather than weeks.

Answer honestly, not hopefully

This only works if you tell it the truth, particularly on the two questions people flinch at: is it true, and does it actually damage me. Wishful answers produce a confident read of a situation you're not actually in, and acting on that is worse than having no plan at all.

Judge it the way a stranger would, not the way it feels at 11pm. Most attacks do not move votes. Some do. The whole point is telling those apart before you spend a response on it.

Safety comes before messaging

You said someone is unsafe. That is not a communications problem and this tool cannot help you with it.

  • Document everything now. Screenshots with dates, messages, names.
  • Report it to law enforcement.
  • Tell your family, and whoever runs your events.

Threats against local officials are common, and most of them come from people who are not physically present. That does not make them harmless. This tool is not legal advice and cannot assess your risk. Talk to law enforcement and a lawyer.

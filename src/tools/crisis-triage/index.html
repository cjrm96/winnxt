Rules, not vibes

Your answers run through a fixed decision model. Nothing here is generated, guessed, or improvised — the same answers always produce the same read, and every branch traces back to something published.

  • Established crisis research. Situational Crisis Communication Theory for matching your response to how much blame you actually carry. Misinformation research for correcting a false claim without spreading it further. Studies on disclosure timing for when to get ahead of a story.
  • Real cases that already ran this experiment. Every outcome comes with people who faced the same decision — what they chose, how the public reacted, and where it ended up. Tylenol, Watergate, the Streisand effect, campaigns that answered too slowly and campaigns that got out in front.
  • Local reality. Calibrated for school board and city council races, where it starts in a neighborhood Facebook group and the whole thing is decided in hours.

Answer honestly, not hopefully

This only works if you tell it the truth — particularly on the two questions people flinch at: is it true, and does it actually damage me. Wishful answers produce a confident read of a situation you're not actually in, and acting on that is worse than having no plan at all.

Judge it the way a stranger would, not the way it feels at 11pm. Most attacks do not move votes. Some do. The whole point is telling those apart before you spend a response on it.

01 / BEFORE YOU START

Go and look first

The failure here is not lying, it is forgetting. Spend twenty minutes gathering before you open the file, and the whole exercise gets better.

  • Search your own name in a private window, signed out, with your city beside it.
  • Open every account you have ever had, including ones you thought were deleted, and read the oldest posts first.
  • Set aside an hour and do it alone. This is not a task to half-finish in a waiting room.

02 / WORK THROUGH IT

Seven sections, 140 prompts

Money, legal, what you said, work, personal, affiliations, your record. Tick what applies. Most people flag two or three per section.

  • Every flag needs three things before the section will let you past: one line describing it, how likely it is to surface, and how bad it is if it does.
  • You will want to stop around section four. That is the exercise working, not a sign to close the tab.

03 / READ THE REGISTER

Three actions, one rule

Your two ratings decide which action applies, using a fixed rule rather than a judgement call. Severity outranks likelihood on purpose.

  • Then run the searches. The register comes with the exact queries and public registers to check, written around your own name.
  • Run the whole thing twice. The second pass, after you have gone looking, is the one that finds things.

04 / KEEP IT

Nothing is saved

Not to a server, not to your browser, not to a file. This is the most sensitive thing this product line asks of anyone, so it leaves nothing behind.

  • Print before you close the tab. Print, or Save as PDF, gives you the register and the case histories.
  • Think before you print. A paper copy of this is a document somebody else could find.

THE THREE ACTIONS

What the register tells you to do

Get ahead of it

Severe, at any likelihood. Say it yourself, first, in your own words, at a time you choose. Worth pre-empting even if you think nobody will find it, because being wrong about that costs the campaign.

Draft a statement

Serious and likely to surface. Do not volunteer it. Have two or three sentences written before you need them, because writing calmly beats writing under pressure.

Prepare an answer

Everything else. Know what you would say, once, so that if it is raised you answer without hesitating. The hesitation is what makes it look like something.

What this cannot do. It cannot pull your records, interview your former business partner, or tell you what your state requires you to disclose. It cannot press you the way a real vetter does, so when an answer comes out of you too fast, that is the moment to ask it again yourself. For anything involving a court record, a settlement, an NDA, or a filing requirement, talk to a lawyer.

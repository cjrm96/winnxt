## Part 1 — What the research says

### Your store, as the market sees it

| | |
|---|---|
| Shop | WINNXTetsy, live since 2024 |
| Sales | 122 |
| Listings | 14 (11 Canva template packs, 3 vinyl stickers) |
| Price band | $9.95–$14.95 digital, $3–$4 stickers |
| Rating | 4.9 from 5 reviews, 13 admirers |

**Three signals worth acting on:**

1. **You already own the endorsement category on Etsy.** You have a "Political Endorsement Canva Graphic Templates" listing. So does one competitor. But *nobody* sells the endorsement **process** — only the announcement graphic. That's a gap you're already positioned in front of.

2. **Your ceiling is $14.95, and that's a self-imposed one.** A reviewer wrote that your templates are "literally the SAME TYPE templates that other political communication firms charge thousands of dollars for." That is a customer telling you your pricing is wrong. Skills are a different value class than a Canva file and can carry $19–$49.

3. **Your one 4-star review is the single most important data point in the whole shop.** "Requires you to purchase canva subscription." That's a dependency complaint. Claude skills have *exactly* the same structural problem — the buyer needs a Claude subscription to use what they bought. Whatever you do, that friction has to be disclosed in the title, the first line of the description, and the first image. Otherwise you trade a 4.9 rating for a 3.8, and on a shop with 5 reviews that's fatal.

### The buyer, in numbers

- **67% of school board members in small districts report their campaign spent under $100 — total.** In large districts, 55% spent over $5,000. The market is bimodal, and the big half is where money exists.
- Realistic local budgets: $1,000–$3,000 low, $3,000–$10,000 competitive, $10,000–$25,000+ for large towns/countywide.
- **~30% of local candidates have zero staff; another 40% have one to three people.**
- The most-valued thing Run for Something gave candidates wasn't training — it was a human who "knew my important deadlines/dates."
- The universal verbatim on fundraising: *"I just hate asking people for money."* Consultants call call time "a candidate's worst nightmare."
- A consultant's rule of thumb: take what a first-time candidate says they'll raise, **cut it in half, then in half again.** That's the real budget.
- Best single quote for your marketing: a first-time candidate wrote, *"We need help. I am a first-time candidate and I only look like I know what I am doing."*

**Read: ~95% of what's for sale in this market is graphics.** The unmet demand is process, sequence, and deadlines. That is precisely what a skill is good at and a template is not.

### What nobody is selling (verified gaps)

1. An endorsement pipeline tool of any kind. Zero. Searches for "endorsement tracker" return generic business spreadsheets.
2. A self-vetting / vulnerability instrument. Every consultant says do it; nobody sells the instrument.
3. A jurisdiction-aware compliance deadline calendar.
4. Structured call-time systems under $100.
5. Candidate forum and debate prep drills — despite public speaking being a top-cited barrier.
6. Anything for the **pre-filing window**, which is where consultants say the fatal errors happen.

### Your crisis framework is the best asset you have

The PDF contains four things that are genuinely proprietary-feeling: the True/False × Hurts/Doesn't-Hurt quadrant matrix, an SCCT crisis-type-to-response mapping, a risk-level × phase planning grid, and a CERC-adapted phase playbook. Crisis comms is the **thinnest competitive space in the entire market** — the only comparable product is a chapter inside a $550 bundle.

**One flag before this ships.** The Offense side of your matrix includes guidance on amplifying unverified claims, retweeting anonymously, and routing through surrogates. Your own document already labels it "High ethical/legal risk." In a retail product sold to strangers under your name, that section is a screenshot waiting to happen, and it's the kind of thing a local reporter writes up. **My recommendation is that the retail version ships defense-only.** Keep the offense matrix as consulting IP for clients who sign something. You lose nothing commercially — candidates buy protection, not attack tooling.

## Part 2 — 29 skill concepts

Difficulty is 1–5 (1 = a weekend, 5 = needs real research or per-state work). Price is what I'd list it at standalone.

### A. Message & identity — highest search volume, universal need

**1. The "Why I'm Running" Builder**
Structured interview that pulls the candidate's actual story out of them, then produces the why in four lengths: a 10-second doorstep version, 30-second, a 2-minute forum answer, and a written version for the website. Tests each draft against "would a voter who disagrees with you still find this credible?"
*Buyer trigger:* "why I'm running for office," "how to explain why I'm running." · *Difficulty 2* · *$19*
Works far better as a skill than a template because the value is entirely in the interrogation. A fill-in-the-blank version produces mush.

**2. Top Three Issues Architect**
Helps pick three issues, which is harder than it sounds. Screens each candidate issue against three filters: does the office actually have authority over it, is it locally salient, and can you speak to it credibly. Kills the issue nobody cares about and the one the board can't legally touch. Outputs three issues with proof points and a one-line frame each.
*Buyer trigger:* "campaign platform," "top three issues," "campaign priorities." · *Difficulty 2* · *$19*
The authority filter is the differentiator — new candidates constantly run on things the seat doesn't control.

**3. Stump Speech Builder**
Takes the why + the three issues and assembles 2-minute, 5-minute, and 10-minute versions with a common spine, so the candidate isn't memorizing three speeches.
*Difficulty 2* · *$19*

**4. Message Discipline Coach / Message Box**
Builds the classic four-quadrant message box (what you say about you, you about them, them about you, them about them), then drills pivoting any hostile question back to the three issues. Includes the "get bored with your message before the voters even hear it" warning.
*Difficulty 3* · *$24*

**5. Announcement Day Package**
Announcement speech, press release, launch social thread, launch email, and a sequenced 72-hour rollout checklist.
*Difficulty 2* · *$24* — natural bundle with your existing Canva launch graphics.

**6. Candidate Bio Ladder**
25-word, 100-word, and 300-word bios plus a third-person press version, all consistent. Feeds ballot statements, websites, endorsement applications, and event intros.
*Difficulty 1* · *$14.95*

**7. Ballot Statement & Voter Guide Writer**
Many jurisdictions give candidates a hard-capped statement in the official voter guide — often 200 or 400 words, sometimes with a per-word printing fee. It is frequently the only campaign material every voter sees. This skill writes to an exact word or character limit and self-counts.
*Difficulty 2* · *$19*
**Underrated. Hard deadline, high stakes, universal, and nobody sells it.**

### B. Crisis & rapid response — your competitive moat

**8. Crisis Triage Matrix** ⭐
Your True/False × Hurts/Doesn't-Hurt quadrant, made interactive. Candidate describes what just happened; the skill asks the diagnostic questions, places it in a quadrant, and returns the response posture — including the times the correct answer is *say nothing*, which is the advice candidates most need and least want.
*Buyer trigger:* "campaign crisis," "how to respond to attack," "opposition attack response." · *Difficulty 3* · *$29–39*

**9. Self-Vet / Vulnerability Audit** ⭐
Runs defensive oppo research on the candidate, on the candidate. Structured interview across the standard vetting scope: finances and debts, legal and civil history, professional discipline, business conflicts, past statements, social media, affiliations. Output is a vulnerability register — each item rated by likelihood of surfacing and severity — plus a pre-drafted holding statement for each one.
*Difficulty 3* · *$29–39*
Every consultant in the field says to do this. Not one person sells the instrument. She Should Run lists "I have skeletons in my closet" as a top barrier to running at all. The framing writes itself: *your supporters can forgive almost anything except being surprised.*

**10. Resurfaced Post Playbook**
Narrow and deep on the single most common way local candidates actually implode: an old tweet or Facebook post surfaces. Includes a pre-emptive audit pass over the candidate's own history, and a graduated response ladder from "delete and ignore" to "get in front of it today."
*Difficulty 3* · *$24*
Two documented local cases: a St. Petersburg council candidate dropped out days after old tweets surfaced; a Norman, Oklahoma council member resigned within three months. The pattern is always local Facebook group first, national amplification second.

**11. Holding Statement Drafter**
For the first 60 minutes. Produces a short, factually safe statement that buys time without conceding anything, in three severity tiers.
*Difficulty 2* · *$19*

**12. Attack Response Decision Tree**
Applies match-the-medium doctrine — hit by mail, answer by mail; hit on TV, answer on TV — with same-day calls and texts as the bridge while production runs. Front-loads the triage question of whether the attack will actually move votes, because most won't.
*Difficulty 3* · *$24*

**13. Apology Architect**
SCCT-driven. Diagnoses the crisis as victim, accidental, or preventable, then routes to diminish, excuse, or rebuild. Prevents the two classic errors: over-apologizing for something that wasn't your fault, and under-apologizing for something that was.
*Difficulty 3* · *$24*

**14. Local Facebook Firestorm Response**
The neighborhood-group-specific playbook: whether to post in the group, whether to comment, how to use surrogates and supporters, when to take it to email or door-knocking instead. Different rules than press, because the audience is 400 neighbors who all know each other.
*Difficulty 3* · *$24*

**15. Harassment & Threat Response**
Reuters documented 200+ instances of school board members harassed or threatened. 73% of tracked incidents came from people not physically present. Covers documentation, when to involve law enforcement, family protection, public statement language, and meeting-day safety.
*Difficulty 4* · *$29*
Real demand, and it's a genuine service. Handle with care and stay well clear of anything resembling legal advice.

### C. Endorsements — the cleanest unbuilt gap

**16. Endorsement Target Map** ⭐
Builds the prioritized list of bodies to pursue for a specific office and geography: central labor councils, building trades, individual locals, county party committees, advocacy orgs, newspaper editorial boards, sitting electeds. Critically, it distinguishes endorsements that deliver money, mail, and member volunteers from the ones that deliver a logo.
*Difficulty 4* · *$29*

**17. Endorsement Answer Bank** ⭐
The same 15–20 policy positions recur across every labor, advocacy, party, and press questionnaire. This builds one canonical set of answers, then re-tailors them per organization. This is the biggest single labor saving in the whole category and it does not exist anywhere.
*Difficulty 3* · *$29*

**18. Questionnaire Responder**
Paste in any endorsement questionnaire; get drafted answers from the answer bank, flagged where a position is risky or where the honest answer will cost the endorsement.
*Difficulty 3* · *$24*

**19. Screening Interview Prep**
Drills the framing labor bodies explicitly reward: "here's what I *know*, what I *believe*, and what I *have done*." Includes mock questions by org type and the personal-connection angle that gets weighted heavily.
*Difficulty 3* · *$24*

**20. Endorsement Sequencing & Rollout**
Endorsers ask you to list your other endorsements — so order matters, and one early local endorsement is nearly irreversible. Maps the dependency chain, sets the pursuit order, drafts the coordinated rollout, and handles post-election accountability follow-through.
*Difficulty 3* · *$24*

### D. Fundraising — where the dread lives

**21. Call Time Prospect Builder** ⭐
Walks the candidate through dumping their entire network from memory — phone contacts, holiday card list, LinkedIn, church, kids' sports, professional associations, alumni — then tiers it and assigns a specific ask amount per person. Produces a working call sheet in priority order.
*Difficulty 3* · *$29*

**22. The Ask — Script & Objection Handler** ⭐
Role-plays the actual call. Handles "let me think about it," "I already gave," "I don't do politics," and the pledge-that-never-arrives. Directly targets the emotional cost per ask rather than producing another finance plan.
*Difficulty 3* · *$29*
The dreaded object isn't the plan, it's the call. Every product in this market sells the plan.

**23. House Party Kit**
Host committee recruiting scripts, invite copy, the 3–5× invite ratio, the three-week lead time, the program script, the ask moment, and follow-up. Includes the warning that a small campaign can lose money on a big event.
*Difficulty 2* · *$24*

**24. Finance Plan Reverse-Engineer**
Works backward from the target budget to number of asks, number of calls, and calls per week — then applies the halve-it-twice reality check to the candidate's own estimate.
*Difficulty 2* · *$19*

**25. PAC & Organization Ask Builder**
Local union and trade association PACs will often give to local candidates, and mostly don't because nobody asked. Identifies which PACs are plausible, drafts the request, and ties it to the endorsement track.
*Difficulty 3* · *$24*

**26. Donor Stewardship Engine**
Thank-you notes within 48 hours, the re-solicit sequence, the maxed-out-donor-as-bundler conversion, and final-week re-asks.
*Difficulty 2* · *$19*

### E. Compliance — high value, handle carefully

**27. Compliance Intake & Deadline Calendar**
Does not state any state's rules. Instead it gives the candidate a "go find these nine facts" worksheet — which authority do I file with, what's my threshold, which forms, what cadence — then builds a dated deadline calendar backward from their election date and hands them the questions to ask their clerk.
*Difficulty 3* · *$29*
This is the safe way to sell compliance, and it delivers the exact thing Run for Something candidates praised most: someone who knew their deadlines.

**28. Money Hygiene Coach**
The layer that actually generalizes across all 50 states: appoint a treasurer *before* touching a dollar, separate bank account, photocopy every check, no cash, log in-kind at fair market value, report gross not net on card donations, never amend informally.
*Difficulty 2* · *$19*
In-kind is where local candidates get caught — donated printing, someone else paying a vendor, personal funds fronting an expense.

**29. Pre-Filing Readiness Check**
The abandoned window. Confirm the exact office title, seat number, district name, election date, filing deadline, signature requirements, and the required disclaimer — *before* spending a dollar on printing. Consultants report candidates paying for materials before confirming the legally required disclaimer, then reprinting everything.
*Difficulty 2* · *$19*
Best lead magnet in the list. Consider giving it away free to build the list.

**A note on state-specific compliance.** I'd steer you away from it. Thresholds range from $1,000 in New York to $2,000 in California to $7,000 in Washington. Filing authority might be the state, the city secretary, or the school district secretary. Florida penalties run $50/day escalating to $500/day, with no extension even if your treasurer is out of town. Publishing a generic dollar figure isn't just unhelpful, it's actively harmful — and a wrong number under your name in a race that gets fined is a liability you don't want on an Etsy listing.

**Bundle all five at $99.** That's the price Campaign Toolkits charges for 89 static graphics. You'd be selling five working systems, and it triples your current basket ceiling.

**Free lead magnet: #29, Pre-Filing Readiness Check.** It reaches people at the moment they're deciding, before they've spent anything, and it's the moment they're most receptive to buying everything else.

### Why not the others
- **Compliance** (27, 28): genuinely valuable, but ship it in round two once the first five prove the format. It's the one category where being wrong costs your customer money.
- **Harassment response** (15): real need, real care required. Not a launch product.
- **Ballot Statement Writer** (7): my sleeper pick. Cheap to build, hard deadline, universal. If you want a sixth, this is it.
- **Stump speech, bio ladder, announcement package** (3, 5, 6): good, but they're downstream of #3 — better as bundle sweeteners than standalone SKUs.

## Part 4 — Three decisions I need from you

**1. Partisan or nonpartisan?** WINNXT is positioned as Republican-focused, and your sticker line is explicitly conservative. But school board and municipal races are frequently nonpartisan, and going nonpartisan roughly doubles the addressable market. You can also run both: keep the stickers and the firm partisan, ship the skills nonpartisan. Note that ActBlue excludes Republicans entirely, so any fundraising skill has to recommend a nonpartisan processor like Anedot or Raise The Money regardless of which way you go.

**2. How do you deliver a skill on Etsy?** A skill is a markdown file. Options: a `.skill` zip the buyer installs, or a plain `SKILL.md` plus a one-page install PDF. Either way the buyer needs a Claude subscription — and your one 4-star review proves this shop's customers will ding you for an undisclosed dependency. This needs to be solved in the listing copy, not after the first complaint.

**3. Does the offense matrix ship?** My recommendation is no. Defense-only in retail; keep offense as client-only consulting IP. Your own PDF flags it as high ethical and legal risk, and it doesn't add commercial value — candidates buy protection.

### One functional constraint to design around

A skill sold as a file can't assume the buyer has connected Gmail, HubSpot, a voter file, or web access. Everything in the top five has to work from conversation alone — structured interview in, drafted artifact out. All five above are designed that way, which is part of why I ranked them where I did. Anything that needs live data (real-time monitoring, voter file lookups, live filing deadlines) will break in a stranger's hands and generate refund requests.

# Etsy listing: Crisis Triage and Rapid Response

Copy for the WINNXTetsy listing. Written to `docs/brand-voice.md`: direct, no
filler, no emoji, and the format limitation disclosed in the title, the first
line of the description, and a product image, exactly as the brand voice rules
require for a dependency.

**Deliberate deviation from the shop workflow.** `skills/winnxt-etsy-listing`
assumes a Canva template: its title formula, its "FREE account on Canva.com"
boilerplate, and its `canva template` / `canva` core tags. None of that applies
here, and using any of it would earn the same one-star surprise as the shop's
existing Canva-subscription review, in reverse. The eight political core tags
are kept. The Canva ones are replaced.

## Title

> Crisis Communication Plan Tool for Political Campaigns & Small Business, Damage Control Decision Guide, Instant Download, No Canva Needed

137 characters, inside Etsy's 140 limit. Front-loaded: "Crisis Communication Plan" is the phrase people
actually type, and Etsy weights the opening of the title most heavily. "No Canva
Needed" is doing disclosure work, not keyword work, and it belongs in the title
because that is where the shop's one bad review came from.

## Description

Crisis communication decision tool for political campaigns, small businesses, and nonprofits. One file that opens in your browser. Not a Canva template, not a printable worksheet, and nothing to install.

Something just happened and you have to decide what to do in the next hour. Answer ten questions, one at a time, and you get one straight answer: whether to respond at all, where, how, and in what order.

WHAT IT TELLS YOU

- Whether to respond. Sometimes the answer is say nothing, and no template will ever tell you that.
- How bad it actually is, on a low to extreme scale, based on how far it spread and how much it damages you with the people you need.
- What to do, in order, from the first hour through the week after it settles.
- What not to do. Most of the damage in a small crisis comes from the response, not the event.
- Where to answer it. A press release does not reach the people who saw a Facebook post.
- How to build the statement, using your own words rather than ours.

WHY IT IS NOT GUESSWORK

Your answers run through a fixed decision model. Nothing is generated or improvised, and the same answers always produce the same read. Every branch traces back to published crisis communication research, cited in the report so you can look it up. Every outcome comes with real cases: Johnson & Johnson, Nixon, Bud Light, BP, Domino's, Pepsi, Kerry, Maple Leaf Foods, Streisand, Romney, Tesla. What they chose, how the public reacted, and where it ended up.

WHAT YOU GET

- crisis-triage.html, the tool. One self-contained file.
- quickstart.pdf, one page. How to open it, what the five verdicts mean, and when to stop and call a professional.

HOW IT WORKS

Download it, double click it, and it opens in Chrome, Edge, Safari, or Firefox on Windows or Mac. It works with the internet switched off, because it never contacts a server. Nothing you type is saved anywhere: no account, no cookies, no file left behind. Close the tab and it is gone, which is the point, and also why you should print your report before you leave.

Built for a desktop or laptop browser. It is not an app and it is not designed for a phone.

WHO IT IS FOR

First-time candidates, local campaigns without a consultant, small business owners, and nonprofit and association staff who have to answer for something publicly and have nobody to call at 11pm.

BE AWARE

This is a communications planning tool, not legal advice. If there is any question of defamation, threats, harassment, or anyone's safety, talk to a lawyer and to law enforcement instead. The tool says so too, and it will stop and tell you when the situation is past what it can help with.

Digital download. Because the file is delivered instantly, this listing is not returnable. Read the format notes above before you buy.

From WINNXT, a political communications firm. This is the same framework we use on real engagements.

## Listing setup

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Digital download |
| Category | Paper & Party Supplies > Paper > Stationery > Design & Templates > Templates |
| Files | `dist/crisis-triage.html`, `dist/quickstart.pdf` |
| Images | `dist/etsy/1-cover.png` first, then 2 through 5, then the shop's branded closing slide |
| Price | Not set. See the pricing note below. |

The category is a compromise. Etsy has no taxonomy entry for interactive
software, and Templates is where these buyers browse, so the listing goes there
and the copy does the work of correcting the expectation. That is the reason
"not a Canva template" appears in the title, the first line, and image five.

There is no buyer PDF with Canva links here, and no Canva share URL to paste.
The customer downloads the two real files directly.

**Pricing is still open.** The relevant considerations: this is not a template
that a competitor can undercut by redrawing it, the research and case library
are the moat, and there is no recurring cost to you per sale. Look at what
crisis communication templates on Etsy currently list for, then price against
what one bad hour costs a candidate, not against what a PDF costs.

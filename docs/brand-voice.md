# WINNXT brand voice

Applies to every word that ships: in-tool copy, listing text, quickstart PDFs, error messages.

**The line:** *"We don't believe in fluff — because neither will your voters."*

## Rules

- **Direct.** Say the thing. No throat-clearing, no "in today's fast-paced political landscape."
- **No jargon.** If a first-time school board candidate wouldn't recognize the word, don't use it. "Rapid response," "earned media," and "GOTV" all need a plain-English gloss the first time.
- **No corporate filler.** No "empower," "unlock," "seamless," "solutions," "journey," "leverage."
- **Sound like a blunt consultant, not a SaaS landing page.** Short sentences. Second person. Willing to tell the buyer something they don't want to hear.
- **Specific over impressive.** "67% of small-district school board members spent under $100 total" beats "campaigns of all sizes."
- **No emoji.** No exclamation points outside genuine warnings.

## Who you're writing to

A first-time candidate with no staff, a budget under $3,000, and a day job. Most of them have never done this before and are quietly worried they'll look foolish. The best available verbatim:

> "We need help. I am a first-time candidate and I only look like I know what I am doing."

Write to that person. Reassure by being competent and clear, not by being cheerful.

## Lines that carry weight (reuse them)

- *Your supporters can forgive almost anything except being surprised.* — the entire self-vet value proposition in nine words.
- Take what a first-timer says they'll raise, cut it in half, then in half again.

## Disclosure is part of the voice

Any dependency — a Claude subscription, a Canva account — gets stated in the listing title, the first line of the description, and the first product image. The shop's one 4-star review ("Requires you to purchase canva subscription") is the canary. Being upfront about a limitation is on-brand; burying it is not.
